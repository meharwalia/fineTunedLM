These are the differences between a brute force attack and a dictionary attack.

**Definition**

- Brute force attack: A trial-and-error method. Hackers guess a password. They input all possible combinations of letters, numbers and symbols until they find the correct one.
- Dictionary attack: A type of brute force attack. The attacker uses a list of common words, phrases and numbers as passwords. The attacker does not generate all possible combinations.

**Speed**

- Brute force attack: Slower than a dictionary attack. It must generate all possible combinations.
- Dictionary attack: Faster than a brute force attack. It tries only a limited number of words.

**Effectiveness**

- Brute force attack: More effective than a dictionary attack. It can guess any type of password, not only words.
- Dictionary attack: Less effective than a brute force attack. It is limited to common words and phrases.

**Detection**

- Brute force attack: Easier to detect. It generates a large number of failed login tries in a short period of time.
- Dictionary attack: Harder to detect. The attacker can slow down the attack. The attacker waits between tries or uses a proxy server.

**Prevention**

- Brute force attack: Use strong and unique passwords. Limit the number of failed login tries. Use two-factor authentication.
- Dictionary attack: Teach users to choose strong and unique passwords. Block the IP address of the attacker.
