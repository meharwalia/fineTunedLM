There are two main types of inflation:

1. **Cost-push inflation:** The prices of goods or services increase because the cost of production increases. Examples are higher raw material costs or labor costs. External factors often cause it, such as changes in the supply of money or increases in taxes.
2. **Demand-pull inflation:** The demand for goods or services is more than the supply. This leads to higher prices. Economic growth, increased consumer spending or low unemployment rates often cause it.

There are also four other types:

1. **Import inflation:** The prices of imported goods or services increase because of changes in exchange rates or tariffs.
2. **Export inflation:** The prices of exported goods or services increase. This decreases the supply of goods or services for domestic consumption.
3. **Inflation expectations:** Consumers and businesses expect inflation to increase in the future. This leads to higher prices in the present.
4. **Built-in inflation:** Prices are set to increase automatically. Examples are contracts with cost-of-living adjustments and industries with high levels of market power.

Other factors also influence inflation. They include government policies, technological advances and global events.
