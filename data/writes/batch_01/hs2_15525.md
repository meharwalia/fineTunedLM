Bipartisan politics in America shows significant polarization. I cannot say for certain that we are more divided than ever. The political landscape has heightened partisanship, especially compared to more consensus-driven eras such as the post-World War II period.

These are five divisive issues:

1. **Immigration:** Recent data shows a reverse trend compared to the earlier stated figures. A Pew Research Center survey shows that 76% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents see immigration as positive for the country. Only 38% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents agree.
2. **Healthcare:** A Kaiser Family Foundation poll shows a stark partisan divide on government-run healthcare. 88% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents support it. Only 18% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents support it.
3. **Gun control:** According to Pew Research Center, 86% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents support stricter gun laws. A minority of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents (31%) favor them.
4. **Climate change:** Pew Research Center data shows that 83% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents see climate change as a major threat. Only 27% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents agree.
5. **Abortion:** A Pew Research Center survey shows that 80% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents say abortion must be legal in all or most cases. Only 38% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents agree.

These issues show how partisan views shape policy debates.

This division is not new in American history. The level of polarization is a unique challenge. Social media and the 24-hour news cycle partly cause it.

The growing politicization of cultural and social issues adds to the divide. Congress reflects these growing partisan differences. Data from reputable sources such as Pew Research Center and Kaiser Family Foundation shows the extent of these divisions.
