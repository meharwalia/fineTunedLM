The Sandinista-Contras conflict was a proxy war in Nicaragua. It was a civil war from 1979 to 1989.

The leftist Sandinista National Liberation Front (FSLN) government fought the right-wing Contras. The United States supported the Contras.

**Cold War background**

- The United States saw Nicaragua as a threat to its interests in the region. Nicaragua was aligned with the Soviet Union and Cuba.
- The United States accused the Sandinista government of support for leftist revolutionary movements in other countries.
- The United States feared that Nicaragua would become a Soviet satellite state.
- The United States then began to support the Contras. They were anti-Sandinista Nicaraguans in exile and former members of the Somoza dictatorship's National Guard.

**Support for each side**

- The Contras got funding, training and weapons from the United States. The Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) and other agencies sent them.
- The United States also imposed an economic embargo on Nicaragua. The embargo weakened the economy and helped the Contras.
- The Soviet Union, Cuba and other leftist governments in the region supported the Sandinista government.

**Result**

The war had a devastating impact on Nicaragua. Thousands of people were killed or displaced.

The war destroyed the economy and infrastructure.

The conflict was a proxy war.

The United States and its allies supported the Contras. The Soviet Union and its allies supported the Sandinista government. It was part of a larger struggle for influence and power in Central America during the Cold War.
