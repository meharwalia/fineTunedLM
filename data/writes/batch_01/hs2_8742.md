To calculate the equivalent resistance between two nodes a knight's move apart on an infinite square grid of resistors of resistance R:

1. Draw a circuit diagram of the square grid and the two nodes. A knight's move is a jump of two squares horizontally or vertically, and two squares diagonally. A knight's move connects the two nodes.
2. Apply the series-parallel rule to the circuit. In a series circuit, the equivalent resistance is the sum of the individual resistances. In a parallel circuit, it is the reciprocal of the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances.
3. Treat the square grid as a square array of resistors. Each resistor connects to four other resistors, two horizontally and two vertically. Apply the series-parallel rule to the array.
4. Consider a single square in the array. It is a series of two resistors, one horizontal and one vertical. Its equivalent resistance is the sum of the individual resistances, or 2R.
5. Treat the entire grid as an infinite number of squares in series. The equivalent resistance of the grid is then the sum of an infinite number of 2R resistors, or 2R * (1 / (1 - 2R)).
6. Add the two resistors that connect the nodes. They are in parallel, so their equivalent resistance is 1 / (1 / R + 1 / R) = R. The equivalent resistance between the nodes is the grid resistance plus this value.
7. The equivalent resistance between the two nodes is 2R * (1 / (1 - 2R)) + R.

This calculation assumes an ideal infinite grid with no defects or impurities. In a real-world situation, imperfections in the material and other factors can change the equivalent resistance.
