The quality of peer review in scholarly papers depends on three factors:

- the expertise and qualifications of the reviewers
- the thoroughness and rigor of the review process
- the transparency and fairness of the evaluation

**Reviewers**

Reviewers must have a strong background in the subject area of the paper. They must also have experience in the research method and publication process.

They must be impartial. They must have no conflicts of interest that can bias their review.

**Review process**

The process includes a review of the method, data analysis and conclusions of the paper. It also includes an evaluation of its novelty, significance and clarity. Reviewers can also check for plagiarism, ethical issues and other publication standards.

**Transparency and fairness**

- Double-blind review improves quality. The reviewers do not know the identities of the authors, and the authors do not know the reviewers.
- Constructive feedback and recommendations for the authors improve quality.

Good peer review makes scholarly research reliable and credible. It also keeps the scientific record intact.
