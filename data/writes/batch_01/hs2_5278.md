If the original estimate of Edwin Hubble was correct, it has significant implications for our understanding of the Andromeda Galaxy. The distance to Andromeda is a crucial factor. It sets the size, structure and properties of the stellar populations of the galaxy.

1. **Apparent size:** A closer galaxy has a smaller apparent size. The angular size of Andromeda is smaller at 900,000 light-years than at 2.537 million light-years, the current estimate. This also changes the estimates of the size of the components of Andromeda: the bulge, the disk and the halo.
2. **Stellar populations:** If the galaxy is closer, its stars appear brighter and more resolved. It is then easier to study their properties in detail. This can give a more accurate age, metallicity and star formation history for the stellar populations of Andromeda.
3. **Distance ladder:** Astronomers use the distance ladder to estimate the distances to other galaxies. A downward revision of the distance to Andromeda can reduce our confidence in the accuracy of the distance ladder. It can also reduce our confidence in the distances to other nearby galaxies.
4. **Distant galaxies:** A downward revision can lead to a revision of the distances to other galaxies in the Local Group. It can also lead to a revision of the distances to galaxies beyond the Local Group. This can affect our understanding of the large-scale structure of the universe and the distribution of matter on the largest scales.

The distance to Andromeda is a critical factor in our understanding of the galaxy and the universe as a whole.
