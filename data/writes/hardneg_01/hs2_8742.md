**Equivalent Resistance Across a Knight's Move on an Infinite Square Grid**

1. It should be noted that a circuit diagram of the square grid and the two nodes is drawn, and a knight's move is a jump that moves two squares horizontally or vertically, and two squares diagonally, by which the two nodes are connected.
2. The series-parallel rule is applied to the circuit, according to which the equivalent resistance of a series circuit is the sum of the individual resistances, while the equivalent resistance of a parallel circuit is the reciprocal of the sum of the reciprocals of the individual resistances.
3. The square grid can be thought of as a series of resistors in a square array, in which each resistor is connected to four other resistors, two horizontally and two vertically, and the equivalent resistance of the grid is calculated by the series-parallel rule being applied to the array.
4. The equivalent resistance of a single square in the array is considered, and the square can be thought of as a series of two resistors, one horizontal and one vertical, so that the equivalent resistance of the square is the sum of the individual resistances, or 2R.
5. The entire square grid can be thought of as an infinite number of squares in series, and therefore the equivalent resistance of the grid is the sum of an infinite number of 2R resistors, or 2R * (1 / (1 - 2R)).
6. The equivalent resistance between the two nodes is the sum of the equivalent resistance of the grid and the two resistors by which the nodes are connected, and since the two resistors are in parallel, the equivalent resistance between the nodes is 1 / (1 / R + 1 / R) = R.

Therefore, the equivalent resistance between two nodes separated by a knight's move of an infinite square grid of resistors of resistance R is 2R * (1 / (1 - 2R)) + R, and it is important to note that this calculation assumes that the grid is an ideal infinite grid with no defects or impurities, whereas in a real-world situation the equivalent resistance may be different due to imperfections in the material and other factors.
