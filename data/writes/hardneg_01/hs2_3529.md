**Basic Rules of Chess**

1. It should be noted that 16 pieces are possessed by each player: 1 king, 1 queen, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, and 8 pawns.
2. The objective of the game is for the opponent's king to be checkmated, which essentially means that the king is put in a position where it is under attack (in check) and there is no way for the king to be moved out of harm's way.
3. Each piece is moved differently: the king moves one square in any direction, the queen moves any number of squares in any direction, the rook moves any number of squares along the same row or column, the knight moves in an L-shape pattern, the bishop moves any number of squares along the diagonals, and the pawn moves one square forward and can capture pieces that are diagonally in front of it.
4. If a player's king is in danger of being checkmated, a move can be made to put the opponent's king in check (under attack) in order to distract them from their own king.
5. Only one move can be made at a time by a player, and a piece cannot be moved into a square that is already occupied by one of their own pieces.
6. If a player's king is in check, it must be moved out of harm's way or the attacking piece must be captured.
7. A special move called "castling" can be made, in which the king is moved two squares toward a rook, and the rook is then moved to the square that the king just crossed.
8. If a player's king is in check and there is no way for the king to be moved or the attacking piece to be captured, the player has no choice but to resign.
9. The game can also be ended in a draw if neither player can make a legal move, or if both players agree to a draw, and numerous other details and variations can affect the strategy of the game.
