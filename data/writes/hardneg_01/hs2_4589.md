**Biometric Systems**

- A biometric system is a technology in which biological characteristics of an individual are utilised in order to identify or verify their identity, and these biometric traits, which are unique to each person, can include features such as facial features, fingerprints, iris patterns, voice, and palm veins.
- Biometric systems are used in a variety of applications, including access control, law enforcement, border control, and healthcare, for example in an access control system a person's fingerprint or iris pattern can be used to grant access to a secure area or building, and suspects or missing persons can be identified in law enforcement.
- It is important to note that several advantages over traditional methods of identification, such as passwords or ID cards, are offered, since biometric traits are unique and difficult to replicate or steal, which makes them a more secure method, and they are also convenient, as the need for passwords or other forms of identification is eliminated.
- However, concerns about privacy and security are also raised, because there are worries that biometric data can be hacked or stolen and used for unauthorized purposes, and some people may be uncomfortable with their biological data being collected and stored.
- In conclusion, a more secure and convenient method of identification than traditional methods is offered, but concerns about privacy and security are also raised.
