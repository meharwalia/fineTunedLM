**Lindy Hop vs. East Coast Swing**

- It is important to note that the Lindy Hop and East Coast Swing are both swing dance styles, but numerous distinct differences exist between them, and essentially the two dances are not performed in the same manner.
- The Lindy Hop is an American dance that originated in the 1920s and 1930s, and it is characterized by its high-energy, athletic style, big, open movements, and a strong connection between partners, and it is danced to fast-paced jazz and big band music.
- The East Coast Swing is basically a simplified version of the Lindy Hop that was subsequently developed in approximately the 1940s, and it is a more structured dance with a basic six-count step pattern, which is typically danced to medium-tempo swing music such as the classic swing sound of the 1930s and 1940s.
- The East Coast Swing is characterized by compact, tight movements, whereas a lot of hopping, swinging, and bouncy movements are involved in both dances.
- Overall, both dances are fun and lively styles that are considered to be great for social dancing and can be enjoyed by dancers of all levels.
