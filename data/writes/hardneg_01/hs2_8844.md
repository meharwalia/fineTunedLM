**Art Nouveau Architecture for a Kid**

- Art Nouveau is a style of art and design that was popular at the turn of the 20th century, and it is characterized by the use of flowing, curved lines and natural forms, such as flowers, leaves, and other plant-inspired shapes.
- It should be noted that Art Nouveau was a reaction against the straight, angular lines of the previous Victorian era, and it was aimed that more organic shapes and patterns would be incorporated into architecture, furniture, jewelry, and other forms of design.
- The style was particularly popular in Europe, and its name comes from the French phrase "art nouveau," which means "new art," and famous examples include the Casa Batlló in Barcelona, Spain, which was designed by Antoni Gaudí, and the Secession Building in Vienna, Austria, which was designed by Joseph Maria Olbrich.
- To a kid, a house or building with lots of curved lines, pretty patterns, and maybe some interesting shapes that make it look different from other buildings might be what Art Nouveau architecture looks like, and unique designs on windows or doors, or a garden or other natural features that make it feel like a special place to be, may be included.
