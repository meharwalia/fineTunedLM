The East Coast and the West Coast of the United States offer different beach experiences. Geographical, climatic, and cultural factors cause the differences.

**East Coast beaches**

1. **Water temperature:** Generally warmer, especially in the Southern Atlantic states. Swimming is comfortable for most of the year.
2. **Sand and shorelines:** The sand often feels softer and finer. The shorelines run wide and long, such as in Florida and the Carolinas.
3. **Ocean conditions:** The Atlantic Ocean tends to have gentler waves in most areas. Surfers know places like the Outer Banks in North Carolina.
4. **Cultural vibe:** Beaches range from historic and quaint, like those in New England, to lively and festive, such as Miami Beach.
5. **Amenities and accessibility:** Most beaches in urban areas have boardwalks, shops, and restaurants.

**West Coast beaches**

1. **Water temperature:** The Pacific Ocean is generally colder. Swimming is less comfortable without a wetsuit, especially in Northern California, Oregon, and Washington.
2. **Sand and shorelines:** The landscapes often look more rugged, with cliffs and rock formations. The sand can be coarser.
3. **Ocean conditions:** The Pacific Ocean has strong currents and waves. They attract surfers, especially in places like California and Hawaii.
4. **Cultural vibe:** Southern California has a laid-back, surf-centric culture. The Pacific Northwest offers more rugged, nature-focused experiences.
5. **Amenities and accessibility:** Developed beaches with amenities exist. Most West Coast beaches are more natural and less commercial.

**Which coast is better?**

The better coast depends on personal preference:

- Choose the East Coast if you prefer warmer water, gentler waves, long sandy beaches, and developed amenities.
- Choose the West Coast if you like surfing, enjoy rugged natural landscapes, and accept cooler water.
