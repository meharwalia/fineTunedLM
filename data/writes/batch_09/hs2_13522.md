Follow these steps to become a nurse in the UK:

1. **Research nursing roles.** The UK has four nursing roles: adult nurse, children's nurse, learning disability nurse, and mental health nurse. Research each role. Choose the one that aligns with your interests and career goals.
2. **Meet the entry requirements.** To study nursing in the UK, you need a good standard of education. The minimum entry requirement is usually five GCSEs at grade C/4 or above, including English, Maths, and Science. Some universities need higher grades or additional qualifications.
3. **Apply for a nursing degree.** When you meet the entry requirements, apply at a UK university. Apply through the Universities and Colleges Admissions Service (UCAS) website.
4. **Complete a nursing degree.** Nursing degrees in the UK usually take three or four years, depending on the course and university. The degree includes theoretical and practical training. This includes placements in healthcare settings.
5. **Register with the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC).** After your degree, you must register with the NMC to practice as a nurse in the UK. Give evidence of your qualifications and identity. Pay a registration fee.
6. **Find a job.** After you register with the NMC, look for nursing jobs in the UK. Search job websites, use recruitment agencies, or contact healthcare providers directly.
7. **Complete ongoing training and development.** To keep your NMC registration, you must do ongoing training. This can include continuing professional development (CPD) courses, workshops, conferences, and further education.

Becoming a nurse in the UK needs a significant amount of education and training. It can be a rewarding and fulfilling career.
