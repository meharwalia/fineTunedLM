Argentina's current poverty results from economic, political, and social factors. The main reasons are:

1. **High inflation.** Argentina has a long history of high inflation. It has reduced the purchasing power of citizens. It has made economic stability difficult.
2. **Debt crisis.** Argentina has had a large debt burden for years. This limits its ability to invest in infrastructure, education, and other areas that are critical for economic growth.
3. **Political instability.** Governments and policies change frequently. This creates uncertainty for businesses and investors. It makes it difficult to attract investment and stimulate economic growth.
4. **Corruption.** Corruption is a persistent problem. It undermines the institutions of the country. It reduces public trust in government.
5. **Dependence on commodity exports.** The economy depends heavily on commodity exports, such as soybeans and wheat. Prices of these goods change in the global market. This makes the country vulnerable to economic downturns. It limits its ability to diversify its economy.
6. **Income inequality.** Argentina has high income inequality. A small wealthy elite exists, and a large number of people live in poverty. This inequality has contributed to social unrest. It has limited sustainable economic growth.

The government, civil society, and the private sector must work together over a long time to solve these challenges. They must implement reforms and policies that promote economic stability, reduce corruption, and create opportunities for all Argentinians.
