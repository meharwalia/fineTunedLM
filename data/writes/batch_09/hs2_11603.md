In postmodernism, a grand narrative is a comprehensive, overarching story or explanation. It tries to account for all aspects of human experience and reality. It gives a framework to understand the past, present, and future.

Grand narratives can be philosophical, ideological, or religious systems. They often include beliefs or values that explain the meaning and purpose of life, the nature of reality, and the direction of history.

Postmodernism critiques grand narratives as overly simplistic and limiting. It argues that no single, objective truth applies to all aspects of human experience. It emphasizes individual perspectives. It also holds that truth is always relative to a particular context or point of view.

Postmodernism encourages people to question and challenge grand narratives. It encourages them to find alternative ways to understand the world. It stresses awareness of one's own biases and assumptions. It also stresses that multiple, conflicting perspectives always exist and need consideration.
