The "food wall" is the point where you suddenly can eat no more. A combination of psychological and physiological factors causes it:

1. **Fullness signals:** A full stomach stretches to its capacity. This activates the stretch receptors in the stomach. They send signals to the brain that you are full. The brain then stops the eating.
2. **Hormones:** Hormones help regulate appetite. When you eat, your body releases hormones such as insulin and ghrelin. They signal to the brain that you are full.
3. **Cognitive factors:** Your beliefs, attitudes, and emotions influence how much you eat. For example, you can believe that you must eat a set amount of food at each meal. You then feel pressure to keep eating even if you are full.
4. **Habits:** Eating habits also play a role. You can habitually eat a set amount of food at each meal. You then feel that you must keep eating even if you are full.
