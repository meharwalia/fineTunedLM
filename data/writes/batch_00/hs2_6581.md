Cancer drugs apply the cell biology of high school to the cancer biology of graduate school.

**Cancer and cancer drugs**

In cancer, cells in the body grow and divide without control. Cancer drugs are medications that treat cancer. They target specific molecules that help cancer cells grow and survive.

**High school**

Students learn the basic concepts of cell biology. These concepts include the cell cycle and the processes of mitosis and meiosis. Students also learn the structure and function of DNA and the mechanisms of genetic inheritance.

**Graduate-level biotechnology and life sciences**

Students learn cancer biology and cancer drug development in depth. They study the molecular and genetic changes in cancer cells. They also study the signaling pathways and cellular processes that cause cancer growth and progression.

**Hallmarks of cancer**

The "hallmarks of cancer" are six essential characteristics of cancer cells:

- sustained proliferation
- resistance to cell death
- limitless replicative potential
- invasion and metastasis
- induction of angiogenesis
- evasion of the immune system

Cancer drugs target specific molecules involved in these hallmarks. Some drugs target proteins in the cell cycle. Other drugs target proteins in signaling pathways that promote cancer growth and survival.

**Types of cancer drugs**

- **Chemotherapy:** This is one of the most common types. It kills fast-dividing cells, including cancer cells.
- **Targeted therapy:** It blocks specific molecules that help cancer grow and survive.
- **Immunotherapy:** It stimulates the immune system to recognize and attack cancer cells.

Scientists develop cancer drugs from a deep understanding of the molecular and genetic changes in cancer cells. By targeting molecules in the hallmarks of cancer, the drugs can treat cancers and improve patient outcomes.
