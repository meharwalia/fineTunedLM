Packet scanning and sniffing are related security concepts. People use them to identify vulnerabilities and monitor network traffic.

1. **Packet scanning:** This technique discovers the devices and services on a network. It sends specially crafted network packets to a target network. It then analyzes the responses to find the types of devices and services. Packet scanning can identify open ports, operating systems, and other network information for security purposes.
2. **Packet sniffing:** This process intercepts and analyzes network traffic to get information about the transmitted data. Packet sniffers are software tools that capture and analyze network packets. The captured information may include the source and destination IP addresses, port numbers, and the contents of the packet, such as the transmitted data. Packet sniffing has legitimate uses, such as the troubleshooting of network issues. It can also serve malicious activities like the theft of sensitive information.

Both techniques have legitimate security uses, such as network security assessments and vulnerability scanning. They can also serve malicious activities. Examples are cyber-attacks, theft of sensitive information, and intrusion into network systems.
