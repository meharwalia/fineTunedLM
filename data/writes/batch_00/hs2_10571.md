Women and Black beginning farmers can use available funding and grants in these ways:

1. Research the available grants and funding opportunities. They exist for women and Black beginning farmers. Choose the ones that fit your farming operation best.
2. Develop a business plan. Grants and funding opportunities often need one. A well-thought-out plan shows that your farming operation is viable. It increases your chances of funding.
3. Seek technical help. Organizations help farmers develop business plans, apply for grants, and manage farming operations.
4. Network with other farmers. They can tell you about funding opportunities. They can also advise you how to apply.
5. Apply for multiple grants. This increases your chances of funding. Make sure each grant matches your farming goals and objectives.
6. Follow up on your applications. Contact the funding organization to confirm that it received your application and is considering it.

These grants and funding opportunities are available:

1. **USDA Beginning Farmer and Rancher Development Program:** It funds education, training, and technical help for beginning farmers and ranchers.
2. **USDA Outreach and Assistance for Socially Disadvantaged Farmers and Ranchers Program:** It funds organizations that give outreach and help to socially disadvantaged farmers and ranchers, including Black farmers.
3. **USDA Value-Added Producer Grant Program:** It helps farmers and ranchers develop new products and markets for their products.
4. **National Institute of Food and Agriculture Beginning Farmer and Rancher Development Program:** It funds education, training, and technical help for beginning farmers and ranchers.
5. **Farm Service Agency Microloan Program:** It gives small loans to beginning farmers and ranchers. They use the loans to start or improve their farming operations.
