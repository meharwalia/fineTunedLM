A discrete event simulation (DES) models the operation of a system as a discrete sequence of events in time. Each event occurs at a specific point in time and changes the state of the system. DES suits systems that have discrete entities that interact in a complex manner.

To use a DES to simulate the consequences of extreme cold in a city:

1. **Define the scope of the simulation.** Decide which aspects of the city to include and which to ignore. For example, simulate the operation of the heating systems of the city. Ignore the traffic flow.
2. **Identify the entities in the system.** The entities can include individual buildings, power plants, and heating systems.
3. **Define the states of the entities.** Define the possible states of each entity. For example, a building can be "heated," "unheated," or "overheated."
4. **Define the events that can occur.** The events can include "power failure," "heating system failure," or "temperature drops below freezing."
5. **Define the rules for transitions between states.** For each event, specify how it affects the states of the entities. For example, if a heating system fails, the building that it serves can change to the "unheated" state.
6. **Implement the simulation.** Use a DES software tool. Implement the entities, states, events, and rules that you defined.
7. **Run the simulation.** See how the system behaves under different conditions. Vary the frequency and severity of extreme cold events. See how they affect the heating systems of the city and the comfort of its inhabitants.
8. **Analyze the results.** Learn the consequences of extreme cold in the city. For example, you can find that specific types of buildings are more vulnerable to heating system failures. You can also find that specific areas of the city are more susceptible to power outages. Use this information to plan strategies that improve the resilience of the city to extreme cold events.
