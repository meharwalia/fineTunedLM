C-reactive protein (CRP) is a protein that the liver produces in response to inflammation in the body. Normal CRP levels are typically less than 0.5 mg/dL. A result of 5.50 mg/dL shows a significant elevation.

An elevated CRP level can show inflammation, infection, or tissue damage. CRP is a non-specific marker. This means that the level can rise in different conditions:

- **Infection:** Bacterial, viral, or fungal infections.
- **Inflammatory conditions:** Rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, and vasculitis.
- **Tissue damage:** For example, after a heart attack or surgery.
- **Cancer:** Certain types of cancer, such as lymphoma or solid tumors.

One elevated CRP level does not show a specific condition. Your doctor considers your CRP result with your symptoms, medical history, and other diagnostic tests. If you have concerns about your CRP level or blood test results, talk to your doctor for further evaluation and guidance.
