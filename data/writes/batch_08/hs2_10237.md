I am not a tax professional, so I cannot give specific tax advice. This is general information about stock options and French taxes.

In France, stock options count as employment income when you exercise them. Capital gains tax applies to gains from the sale of the shares.

The rate depends on how long you hold the shares. The rate is lower after two years.

You have four strategies:

- **Exercise and hold:** Exercise the options. Hold the shares for at least two years before you sell them. You get the lower capital gains tax rate. You pay income tax on the value of the shares at exercise.
- **Exercise and sell:** Exercise the options. Sell the shares immediately. This triggers income tax on the value of the shares at exercise. You avoid capital gains tax if you sell within two years.
- **Exercise and sell to cover:** Exercise the options. Sell enough shares to cover the income tax. You keep the remaining shares. They can gain value in the future.
- **Participate in the ESPP:** The Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP) lets you buy shares at a discount. You can sell the shares immediately and pay income tax on the discount. You can also hold the shares and pay capital gains tax on any gains.

The tax rules for stock options and ESPPs are complex. Other factors can apply to your situation. Talk to a tax professional who knows the French tax system.
