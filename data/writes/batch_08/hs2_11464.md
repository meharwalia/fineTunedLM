People in Taiwan follow seven traditions to let go of bad luck and bring in good fortune:

1. **Spring cleaning:** Before the Lunar New Year, Taiwanese families clean their homes thoroughly. They sweep away bad luck and make room for good fortune.
2. **Incense and prayers:** People visit temples to pray for good luck and burn incense. They believe the smoke carries prayers to the heavens.
3. **Red envelopes:** During the Lunar New Year, people give and receive red envelopes that contain money. The red color symbolizes good luck. People believe it wards off evil spirits.
4. **Lucky foods:** People eat specific foods during the Lunar New Year. Fish is lucky because the Chinese word for fish sounds like the word for surplus. Dumplings are lucky because they look like ancient Chinese silver or gold ingots. Niangao (glutinous rice cake) symbolizes higher income or position.
5. **Firecrackers and fireworks:** People believe the loud noise scares away evil spirits and brings in good luck.
6. **Peach wood:** People believe peach wood has the power to ward off evil spirits. A few people carry peach wood talismans. Others place peach wood swords at their doors.
7. **Feng Shui:** Taiwanese families follow the principles of Feng Shui. They arrange their homes and offices to bring good luck and positive energy.

These are cultural practices and beliefs. Their effectiveness is a matter of personal belief.
