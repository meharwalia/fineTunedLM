Breath biomarkers are volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in a person's exhaled breath. Doctors can measure them. They have the potential to be non-invasive diagnostic tools for diseases, including diabetes.

Research on breath biomarkers for diabetes is ongoing. Researchers suggest four VOCs as potential markers for different types of diabetes:

1. **Acetone:** High levels of acetone in exhaled breath relate to poor glycemic control in Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes. When the body breaks down fat for energy because of insufficient insulin, it produces acetone as a byproduct.
2. **Isoprene:** Isoprene relates to glucose metabolism. Studies suggest that exhaled isoprene levels can be lower in Type 1 diabetes patients than in healthy individuals. This suggests that it is a potential biomarker for this condition.
3. **Pentanal:** Pentanal relates to oxidative stress, a characteristic feature of diabetes. Studies suggest that pentanal levels can be higher in the exhaled breath of Type 2 diabetes patients. This suggests that it is a potential biomarker for this condition.
4. **2-Pentanone:** 2-Pentanone relates to lipid peroxidation. Lipid peroxidation occurs as a result of oxidative stress. Studies suggest that 2-pentanone levels can be higher in the exhaled breath of Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes patients. This suggests that it is a potential biomarker for diabetes.

These VOCs show promise as breath biomarkers for diabetes. Researchers need more research to validate them as a reliable diagnostic tool.

Diet, smoking, and medication use also affect VOC levels in exhaled breath. Future studies must consider these factors.
