Yes, you can index the costs of the redevelopment of a property in the UK. You then offset the indexed amount against the taxable gain on the final sale. Section 24 of the Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992 outlines the rules.

You can offset the cost to acquire and improve the property against the sale proceeds. This reduces the taxable gain. The cost of improvements after 5 April 1998 qualifies only if they are "relevant improvements".

Relevant improvements increase the value of the property. They are of a capital nature. Examples are building or renovating the property and installing permanent fixtures and fittings.

To index the losses, adjust the cost of the relevant improvements for inflation with the Retail Price Index (RPI). This stops inflation from eroding the value of the losses. You then offset the indexed cost against the taxable gain on the sale.

The indexing of losses is a complex area of tax law. The rules can change.
