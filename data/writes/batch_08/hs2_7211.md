The Chinese Room Experiment is a thought experiment. The philosopher John Searle first proposed it in 1980.

It shows the limitations of artificial intelligence (AI). It also shows that machines cannot truly understand language.

A person, the "interactor", is locked in a room. The interactor gets a set of instructions in English. The interactor responds to written messages from people outside the room.

The messages are also in English. The interactor does not speak English. The interactor has a set of rules and a database of responses in Chinese.

The interactor uses them to generate responses. The people outside the room believe they communicate with a native Chinese speaker.

The responses can seem meaningful and relevant. The interactor still does not understand Chinese or the meaning of the messages.

The experiment shows the difference between mechanical manipulation of symbols and genuine understanding. The rules and responses of the interactor are the mechanical manipulation.

Searle wanted to challenge the idea that AI can achieve true intelligence or understanding. He argued that machines, unlike human beings, cannot have beliefs, desires, or intentions.

They cannot have genuine understanding or consciousness. They only generate responses based on patterns they learned in training or rules that people programmed.

Philosophy, artificial intelligence, and cognitive science have debated the experiment much. Certain people argue that it shows the limitations of current AI technology. Others argue that it is a flawed argument.

They say it ignores the ongoing progress in AI. The experiment remains an important and influential thought experiment in AI and the study of human cognition.
