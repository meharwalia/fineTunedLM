NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization) is a military alliance established in 1949. It has 30 member countries from Europe and North America. They include the United States, Canada, and European Union (EU) member states.

NATO promotes peace, security, and stability in the North Atlantic region. It provides a collective defense mechanism and fosters cooperation on security issues.

The founding members were mainly concerned about the Soviet Union threat during the Cold War. They created the alliance to deter Soviet aggression and to defend collectively against attacks on any member.

Since then, NATO has expanded its mission and scope. It now includes terrorism, cyber warfare, and hybrid threats.

Today, NATO is a key player in international security affairs. It focuses on collective defense, crisis management, and cooperative security. It works with other international partners and organizations to address global security challenges and promote peace and stability around the world.

**Key features**

- Collective defense: An attack on one member is an attack on all members. The alliance commits to give military assistance to any attacked country. Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty states this commitment.
- Cooperation on security issues: NATO coordinates security initiatives with its members, such as for terrorism, cyber warfare, and hybrid threats. It shares intelligence and resources.
- Military operations: NATO has run peacekeeping missions, humanitarian relief efforts, and combat operations. It has deployed troops to Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Afghanistan, and Libya. The United Nations (UN) or the North Atlantic Council, the decision-making body of NATO, usually authorizes these operations.
- Political dialogue: NATO holds regular meetings and summits with its members and other international partners. They discuss security challenges and develop strategies.
- International partnerships: NATO has partnerships with countries and organizations such as the EU, the UN, and the African Union (AU). These partnerships let NATO collaborate on security initiatives and share resources and expertise.

NATO continues to evolve and adapt to new security challenges. It stays committed to the safety and security of its member countries and the broader North Atlantic region.
