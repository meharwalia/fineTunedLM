Follow these steps to become a registered nurse (RN) in Maryland:

1. Earn a high school diploma or equivalent. A strong foundation in science and mathematics is important for nursing studies.
2. Complete an accredited nursing program. In Maryland, you can become an RN with an Associate's Degree in Nursing (ADN) or a Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BSN). These programs typically include classroom and clinical instruction.
3. Apply for licensure. After the program, apply through the Maryland Board of Nursing. You can apply online or by mail.
4. Pass the National Council Licensure Examination (NCLEX-RN). After the board processes your application, you receive authorization to take the exam. It tests the knowledge and skills for safe and effective practice as a newly licensed, independent RN.
5. Complete a criminal background check. This is part of the licensure process. It requires fingerprinting at specified locations throughout Maryland.
6. Meet continuing education requirements. To maintain your RN license, complete the requirements that the Maryland Board of Nursing establishes. This typically includes a specified number of hours every two years.
7. Renew your license. You must renew it every two years. You can renew online through the Maryland Board of Nursing's website.
