Here are five natural and five artificial carbon sinks.

**Natural carbon sinks**

1. Forest: Trees and other vegetation absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere through photosynthesis. They store it in their tissues, roots, and soil. This reduces atmospheric carbon dioxide and helps mitigate climate change.
2. Ocean: The ocean is a significant natural carbon sink. It absorbs carbon dioxide from the atmosphere through chemical reactions in the water. This helps regulate the Earth's climate. The ocean stores the excess carbon dioxide in the deep ocean.
3. Soil: Soil stores carbon in its organic matter. This reduces atmospheric carbon dioxide and helps mitigate climate change. Soil also plays a vital role in the cycling of nutrients and the maintenance of biodiversity.
4. Wetlands: Wetlands, such as marshes, swamps, and mangroves, store carbon in their soil and vegetation. This reduces atmospheric carbon dioxide and helps mitigate climate change. Wetlands also provide important habitat for wildlife.
5. Peatlands: Peatlands, such as bogs and wetlands, store carbon in their soil. They store large amounts of carbon, so they are important for mitigating climate change. Their preservation helps reduce atmospheric carbon dioxide.

**Artificial carbon sinks**

1. Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS): This technology captures carbon dioxide emissions from industrial sources, such as power plants and factories. It stores them underground in geological formations. This reduces atmospheric carbon dioxide and helps mitigate climate change.
2. Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS): This technology combines renewable energy production, such as bioenergy, with carbon capture and storage. It produces clean energy and removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. This helps mitigate climate change.
3. Afforestation: This is the planting of new forests on land that was barren or used for other purposes. It increases the carbon stored in forests and reduces atmospheric carbon dioxide. This helps mitigate climate change.
4. Soil carbon sequestration: This process enhances carbon storage in soil through management practices, such as crop rotation, cover cropping, and conservation tillage. It reduces atmospheric carbon dioxide and helps mitigate climate change.
5. Ocean iron fertilization: This technology adds iron to the ocean to stimulate the growth of algae. The algae absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. This increases the carbon stored in the ocean and helps mitigate climate change.

Artificial carbon sinks can help mitigate climate change. They are not a substitute for reducing greenhouse gas emissions and transitioning to a low-carbon economy.

Natural carbon sinks, such as forests, oceans, and soil, are essential for maintaining a stable climate. Protect and enhance them as much as possible.
