Councils shaped history and church doctrine throughout the centuries. A council is a formal gathering of church leaders, theologians, and other experts. They discuss and decide important issues that affect the church and its teachings.

Councils shaped church doctrine and resolved disputes within the church. For example, the First Council of Nicaea in 325 AD addressed Arianism, a teaching that denied the divinity of Jesus Christ. The council produced the Nicene Creed.

The creed affirmed the divinity of Jesus and established a clear doctrine of the Trinity.

Councils also shaped the course of events and influenced the development of societies. For example, the Council of Trent in the 16th century AD addressed the Reformation, a movement that challenged the authority of the Catholic Church.

The council's teachings and decisions solidified the doctrine and influence of the Catholic Church. They shaped the course of European history and influenced the development of societies across the continent.

A specific example of this impact is the Second Vatican Council, held in the 1960s AD. It addressed the changing social and cultural landscape of the world. It updated the church's teachings and practices to better meet the needs of modern society.

Its decisions and teachings had a significant impact on the church. They changed the liturgy, the doctrine, and the church's relationship with the wider world.
