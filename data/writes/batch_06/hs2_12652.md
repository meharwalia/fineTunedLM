The Pledge of Allegiance is a patriotic oath. People in the United States traditionally recite it to show loyalty and respect for the American flag and the country it represents.

Francis Bellamy, a socialist minister, wrote it. It was first published in 1892 as:

> "I pledge allegiance to my Flag and the Republic for which it stands, one nation, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all."

- 1923: The words "my Flag" changed to "the Flag of the United States" to make the pledge more inclusive.
- 1954: An act of Congress added the words "under God". President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed it into law.

The current version is:

> "I pledge allegiance to the flag of the United States of America, and to the republic for which it stands, one nation under God, indivisible, with liberty and justice for all."

People recite it at public events, in schools, and at government meetings. It symbolizes American patriotism and unity.
