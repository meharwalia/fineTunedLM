Heuristics are mental shortcuts, or rules of thumb, that people use to make decisions, solve problems, or form judgments quickly and efficiently. They rely on past experiences, assumptions, and intuition. They do not rely on a systematic analysis of all available information.

Heuristics are useful in many situations. They let us make quick decisions without spending much time on the details.

However, they can also lead to errors and biases. They may make us overlook important information or make assumptions that facts do not support.

Common heuristics include:

- Availability heuristic: Estimating the likelihood of an event based on how easily examples come to mind.
- Representativeness heuristic: Judging the likelihood of an event based on how closely it resembles a stereotype or prototype.
- Anchoring heuristic: Relying too heavily on an initial piece of information when making later judgments.

Psychology studies heuristics, particularly in judgment and decision-making. Researchers found that people often use heuristics automatically and unconsciously. These shortcuts can have a significant impact on decisions.
