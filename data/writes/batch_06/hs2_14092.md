Encapsulating payload security, also known as payload encryption, is the process of encrypting the payload of a communication session. It protects the data from unauthorized access during transmission over a network. It usually uses an encryption protocol, such as SSL/TLS, to wrap the data in an encrypted packet and send it over the network.

It prevents attackers from intercepting and reading the transmitted data, even if they can access the network. This matters because firewalls and intrusion detection systems inspect and filter traffic based on packet headers and other metadata. They do not inspect the transmitted data.

You can use it to protect data in applications such as web browsing, email, and file transfer. It is particularly important for sensitive data, such as personal information, financial data, and proprietary information.

Even if an attacker compromises the network, the data stays private and secure.
