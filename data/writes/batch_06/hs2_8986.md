University licensing practices can contribute to the high price of modern medicines in these ways:

1. Exclusive licensing: Universities may grant exclusive licenses to pharmaceutical companies to develop and commercialize drugs discovered through academic research. An exclusive license gives the licensee (the pharmaceutical company) the sole right to develop and sell the drug. This can lead to higher prices, because there is no competition.
2. Royalty payments: Universities may receive royalty payments from pharmaceutical companies for licensed drugs. Royalties are typically a percentage of the licensee's sales revenue and can be substantial. A high royalty rate may encourage the licensee to set a high price to maximize profits.
3. Limited negotiation power: Universities may have limited negotiation power for licensing terms, particularly if they depend on licensing income to support their research. The terms can favor the licensee (the pharmaceutical company). They may not address concerns about affordability and access.
4. Lack of consideration for public health: Universities often aim to commercialize research findings to benefit society. This may not consider the broader impact on public health, including the affordability and accessibility of the medicine.

These factors can contribute to the high prices of modern medicines. They make it challenging for certain patients to access the treatments they need.
