You can avoid probate in these ways:

1. Beneficiary designations: Complete a beneficiary designation form to name beneficiaries for assets such as bank accounts and investment accounts. These assets pass directly to the beneficiary when you die. They do not go through probate.
2. Living trust: A living trust is a legal entity. It holds your assets during your life and passes them to your beneficiaries after your death. Transfer ownership of your assets to the trust. Name a successor trustee to manage the trust assets for your beneficiaries. A living trust needs ongoing maintenance. It can also have higher legal fees than other options.
3. Joint ownership: Own assets such as real estate and bank accounts jointly with another person. When one owner dies, the asset passes automatically to the surviving owner. It does not go through probate.
4. Payable-on-death (POD) or transfer-on-death (TOD) designations: Name a beneficiary for assets such as bank accounts and investment accounts. The asset passes directly to the beneficiary when you die. It does not go through probate.
5. Small estate affidavit: In a few states, if the estate value is below a set threshold, the beneficiaries can file a small estate affidavit. They then claim the assets without probate.

Each state has its own probate laws. The best way may depend on the circumstances of your estate. Consult a qualified estate planning attorney to find the best options for your situation.
