To design a NAND gate in a 0.35µm process, select the widths of the pMOS and nMOS transistors. The rise and fall times of the output signal depend on the transconductance and output resistance of the circuit. These depend on the dimensions of the transistors.

Follow these steps to select the widths:

1. Find the drive capability that the NAND gate needs. It depends on the input signals and the load that the output drives.
2. Choose a suitable technology file for the 0.35µm process. It gives information on the channel length, oxide thickness, and other parameters of the transistors.
3. Based on the drive capability you need, select a suitable number of stages to connect in series to form the NAND gate. For example, use a two-stage NAND gate with a primary stage and a secondary stage.
4. For the primary stage, use a pMOS transistor with a width (W) of 4µm and a length (L) of 1µm. This gives the drive capability you need and a reasonable gain.
5. For the secondary stage, use an nMOS transistor with a width (W) of 2µm and a length (L) of 1µm. This gives the amplification you need and a reasonable gain.
6. Use a resistor of 100-200Ω to limit the output current and improve the noise margin.
7. Simulate the NAND gate with a SPICE simulation tool to verify the performance. Adjust the widths if necessary.

With these transistor widths, you should get an equal rise and fall time. Actual performance may vary with the specific process and other design considerations.
