A strained intercostal muscle is one of the most common causes of a sharp pain on the left side just under the bottom rib when you breathe deeply or sneeze. The intercostal muscles are the muscles between the ribs. Overuse, coughing, vomiting or rapid movements that make the ribs expand quickly can strain them.

Costochondritis is another possible cause. It is an inflammation of the cartilage that connects the ribs to the sternum (breastbone).

Overuse, trauma or infections can cause it. It can cause sharp pain with deep breathing or coughing.

Less common causes of left-sided pain with deep breathing or sneezing:

- Pneumonia or other lung infections
- Pulmonary embolism (a blood clot in the lung)
- Heart attack (although you are more likely to feel the pain in the center or left side of the chest)
- Kidney infection or kidney stones
- Appendicitis (although you are more likely to feel the pain in the lower right abdomen)

If the pain is severe or persistent, get medical attention promptly. Do the same if other symptoms accompany the pain, such as fever, chest tightness or sweating. These can be signs of a more serious underlying condition.
