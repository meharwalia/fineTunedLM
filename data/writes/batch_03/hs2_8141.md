IoT protocols let the physical devices, vehicles, home appliances and other objects in the Internet of Things (IoT) connect to the internet, communicate and exchange data. These are the most common IoT protocols:

1. **IPv6 over Low Power Wireless Personal Area Networks (6LoWPAN):** This protocol suits low-power, low-data-rate devices that communicate over short distances. Home automation and smart city applications use it. It lets devices communicate over IPv6 networks. IPv6 gives a larger address space than older protocols. It also gives better routing.
2. **Message Queue Telemetry Transport (MQTT):** This lightweight, publish-subscribe protocol is widely used in IoT applications. Devices publish data to a central server. Other devices or applications then use the data to take action or display information.
3. **Constrained Application Protocol (CoAP):** This lightweight protocol suits constrained devices. It is similar to HTTP. It is optimized for devices with limited resources, such as low memory and processing power.
4. **Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN):** This protocol suits devices that collect and transmit data over a wireless network, such as temperature sensors or motion detectors. IoT applications that need a large number of communicating devices often use it.
5. **Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP):** This is the most widely used protocol on the internet. Also, IoT applications use it. It lets devices communicate with servers and other devices over the internet. It often transfers data and control commands.

Each protocol has its own strengths and weaknesses. The choice of protocol depends on the requirements of the application.
