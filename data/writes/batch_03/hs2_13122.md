A promissory note is a legal document. In it, one party promises to pay another party a set amount of money at a specified future time, usually with interest.

To create a legally binding Philippine promissory note:

1. Identify the parties. Name the borrower (who promises to pay) and the lender (who is owed money).
2. Specify the loan amount. State clearly the amount of money borrowed.
3. Determine the interest rate. State the rate for the loan. The rate can be fixed or variable.
4. Set the repayment schedule. State the dates and amounts of the repayments. You can schedule them monthly, quarterly or annually.
5. Include default provisions. State what happens if the borrower pays late or defaults on the loan. These can include late fees, acceleration of the remaining balance or other penalties.
6. Add signatures and witnessing. The borrower and the lender sign the note. A neutral party witnesses it.
7. Make copies. Each party keeps a copy of the signed note.

A promissory note is a legal document. Ask a lawyer or other legal professional for advice, to make sure the note is properly drafted and legally binding.
