If the U.S. government had let the American auto industry fail in 2010, with no interventions, it would have had far-reaching consequences for the economy and society. These are possible outcomes:

1. **Job loss:** The auto industry employs millions of workers directly and indirectly. Its collapse would have caused massive job losses and economic hardship for families.
2. **Supply chain disruption:** The auto industry is a major buyer of raw materials, components and services from other industries. Its failure would have caused significant supply chain disruptions. It could have closed other businesses.
3. **Decreased economic activity:** The auto industry contributes directly and indirectly to economic activity. Its collapse would have significantly decreased economic activity. This would have weakened the economy further.
4. **Decreased tax revenue:** The auto industry is a major source of tax revenue for state and local governments. Its collapse would have decreased tax revenue. It could have led to cuts in public services.
5. **Increased unemployment:** The collapse would have increased unemployment. This would have weakened the economy further. It could have led to social unrest.
6. **Decreased innovation:** The auto industry is a major driver of innovation in product development and manufacturing processes. Its collapse would have decreased the ability to innovate. It could have slowed technological progress.
7. **Decreased consumer confidence:** The collapse would have significantly affected consumer confidence. This would have weakened the economy further. It could have led to a downward spiral of economic activity.

The failure of the American auto industry in 2010 would have had far-reaching and long-lasting consequences for the economy and society.
