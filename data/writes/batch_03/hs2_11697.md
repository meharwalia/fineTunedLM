Behavioral law and economics is an interdisciplinary field. It combines insights from behavioral economics and psychology with traditional legal analysis. It gives a more nuanced understanding of human behavior and decision-making in the context of the law.

The field studies how people actually behave when they face legal and regulatory choices. It does not assume that people act in a rational, self-interested manner.

It pays attention to the cognitive biases, heuristics and social influences that affect decision-making. It seeks to develop legal frameworks that account for these factors.

The field has implications for legal issues such as contract law, torts, criminal law and regulatory policy. For example, its insights can help design better contracts. These contracts make the terms more understandable and easier to compare.

Its insights can also help develop criminal justice reform policies. These policies better match how people make decisions under stress or fear.

The field seeks a more realistic and holistic understanding of human behavior in legal settings. Its goal is to create more effective and efficient legal institutions.
