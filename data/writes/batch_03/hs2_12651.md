The method is "Gini importance", also called "mean decrease in impurity".

Gini importance measures how much a feature contributes to the homogeneity of the nodes and leaves in the decision tree. It is the weighted average of the Gini impurity decrease for each feature. The weights are the proportion of samples that reach each node.

Gini impurity measures how often a randomly chosen element from a set gets an incorrect label. This happens when you label it randomly, with the distribution of labels in the set.

The feature with the highest Gini importance contributes the most to the homogeneity of the nodes and leaves. So it is the most important feature.
