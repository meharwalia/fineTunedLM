Wittgenstein's theory of meaning is a philosophical concept. The Austrian philosopher Ludwig Wittgenstein developed it in the early 20th century. It says that the use of a word or phrase in a particular context gives its meaning. Inherent properties and the intentions of the speaker do not give it.

According to Wittgenstein, you cannot understand the meaning of a word in isolation. Its role in a particular language game gives the word its meaning. A language game is a set of rules and conventions for how people use words in a particular context.

For example, the word "dog" has a different meaning for a pet, for a breed of dog, and in a metaphor such as "life is a dog."

Wittgenstein believed that language and thought are deeply intertwined. He believed that the use of words in different contexts shapes their meaning. He argued that you cannot reduce the meaning of a word to a set of innate or inherent properties. It is a product of how people use the word in a particular context.

The theory has had a significant effect on philosophy, linguistics, and other fields. People still study and debate it widely today.
