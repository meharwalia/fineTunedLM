The verb form of propaganda is "propagandize." It means to spread propaganda. It also means to use propaganda techniques to influence the opinions or behavior of others.

**Meaning and use**

- People often use the term in a negative or pejorative sense. It describes the deliberate spread of false or misleading information. The purpose is to manipulate public opinion or to get a political or commercial advantage.
- Propagandizing has different forms, including advertising, public relations, and political campaigning.
- It can use mass media, such as television, radio, and the internet. It can also use targeted methods, such as personal communication and social media.
- The goal is typically to persuade people to adopt a point of view, to support a cause or policy, or to take a specific action.
- Propagandizing can also have legitimate purposes, such as raising awareness about important issues or promoting social change.

The use of propaganda is a complex and controversial topic. People widely debate its ethical and moral implications in many different contexts.

**Examples**

- People accuse the government of propagandizing the public to justify its military actions.
- The company has a reputation for aggressive propagandizing techniques. They aim to persuade consumers to buy its products.
- The organization uses propaganda to raise awareness about the issue of environmental conservation and to encourage people to take action to protect the planet.
- People accuse the political candidate of using propaganda to manipulate public opinion and to gain support for their campaign.
- Critics blame the media for biased reporting and for propagandizing the public to advance a particular agenda.
