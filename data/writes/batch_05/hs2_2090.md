Specifications are hard to read because multiple factors affect the speed of a computer. Clock speed is the frequency at which the core of the processor operates. Advertisements often show it. It is not the only factor that sets the speed of a computer.

A higher clock speed generally shows a faster computer. Other factors also affect performance:

- The number of cores
- The speed and capacity of memory
- The efficiency of the operating system and software

Different applications and tasks have different performance requirements. A computer that is fast for one task can be slow for another.

Moore's Law says that the number of transistors on an integrated circuit doubles about every two years. It has driven the rapid advance of computer technology for decades. It mainly refers to the physical limits of manufacturing integrated circuits. It does not directly relate to the performance of individual computers.

Better manufacturing allowed higher clock speeds and additional cores. The speed and capacity of memory also improved. Together these improvements increased overall computer performance.

To understand how specifications affect practical and functional performance, consider these factors:

- The architecture of the processor
- The amount and speed of memory
- The efficiency of the operating system and software
- The specific tasks that the computer will do

Reviews and benchmarks from reputable sources also help. They give a more complete understanding of the performance of a computer.
