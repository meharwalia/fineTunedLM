The concept of derivatives comes from the study of motion and change.

**The problem**

In the early 17th century, scientists began to observe and measure the motion of objects. Examples are the speed of a falling object and the rate of flow of a river. They found that the motion of an object is not always constant. The speed or flow rate of an object can change over time.

To understand and predict this motion, scientists needed a way to measure the rate of change of the speed or flow rate of an object. This led to the concept of the derivative. A derivative measures the rate of change of a function or variable over a specific interval.

**What a derivative means**

The derivative of a function measures the instantaneous rate of change of the function at a particular point. To calculate it, find a limit. The limit is the ratio of the change in the function value to the change in the input value. The input value approaches a specific point.

You can think of the derivative as the slope of the tangent line to the function at that point.

**Uses**

The derivative has practical applications in physics, engineering, economics, and finance. People use it to solve problems about motion, optimization, and the analysis of rates of change. The derivative is also a fundamental concept in calculus. Calculus is the branch of mathematics that studies change and the properties of functions.
