To create a civil lawsuit, follow these steps:

1. **Find out if you have a valid legal claim.** You need evidence that the other party caused you harm or damage. You also need evidence that you have a right to compensation or relief.
2. **Send a demand letter.** Send it to the other party before you file a lawsuit. State your legal claim, the damages you suffered, and your demand for relief. The letter can resolve the dispute without a lawsuit.
3. **File a complaint.** If the demand letter does not succeed, file a complaint with the court. State your legal claim in it. State the facts that support your claim. State the relief that you want.
4. **Serve the complaint.** After you file the complaint, serve a copy to the other party. Use a sheriff, a private process server, or certified mail.
5. **Respond to the complaint.** The other party has a set amount of time to respond. They can admit the allegations, deny them, or raise defenses.
6. **Discovery.** After the first responses, the parties can exchange information and evidence. This process is discovery. It can include depositions, document requests, and interrogatories.
7. **Settlement negotiations.** Before trial, the parties can negotiate a settlement. This can resolve the dispute without a trial.
8. **Trial.** If settlement negotiations do not resolve the case, it goes to trial. Both parties present their evidence and arguments to a judge or jury.
9. **Judgment.** After the trial, the judge or jury gives a decision. This decision is a judgment. It decides who is liable for the damages. It decides which relief is appropriate.
10. **Appeal.** A party that is not satisfied with the outcome can appeal the judgment. A higher court reviews the decision of the trial court.

A civil lawsuit can be a complex process. Get the advice of an experienced attorney to help you use the legal system.
