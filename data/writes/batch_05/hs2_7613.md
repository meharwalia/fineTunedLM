Balanced harvesting is a fisheries management strategy. It aims to keep a healthy and sustainable fish population. It has five key concepts:

1. **Stock assessment.** The first step is to understand the size and health of the fish population. This needs regular monitoring and assessment of the fish stock. The assessment includes fishing pressure, habitat quality, and other factors that affect the population.
2. **Fishing quotas.** The stock assessment sets fishing quotas. They ensure a sustainable harvest of the fish population. Quotas are typically a percentage of the total fish population. They keep a healthy balance between the need for fishing and the need to protect the population.
3. **Management regulations.** Regulations ensure that fishing is sustainable. They can include limits on the number of fish that fishers can catch. They can include size limits on the fish that fishers can harvest. They can include seasonal closures. They protect the fish population. They ensure that fishing activities do not harm the ecosystem.
4. **Bycatch and discards.** Bycatch is fish that fishers catch by accident. They do not intend to harvest it. Balanced harvesting aims to minimize bycatch and discards. It aims to maximize the sustainable harvest of target species.
5. **Economic considerations.** Balanced harvesting also considers the economics of the fishing industry. Fishing must be a viable livelihood for fishermen. The fish population must also stay healthy and sustainable.

Balanced harvesting aims to balance the need for fishing and the need to protect the fish population and the ecosystem. It combines stock assessment, management regulations, and economic considerations. This helps keep fisheries sustainable for future generations.
