**Creating a Legally Binding Philippine Promissory Note**

A promissory note is a legal document that contains a promise by one party to pay another party a certain amount of money, usually with interest, at a specified time in the future, and the steps below should be followed in order to create a legally binding Philippine promissory note.

1. **Identify the parties:** The borrower (the party that promises to pay) and the lender (the party that is owed money) should be identified in the note.
2. **Specify the loan amount:** The amount of money that is being borrowed should be clearly stated.
3. **Determine the interest rate:** The interest rate that will be charged on the loan should be specified, and it can be fixed or variable.
4. **Set the repayment schedule:** The dates and amounts of the scheduled repayments should be specified, and the schedule can be structured in various ways, such as monthly, quarterly, or annually.
5. **Include default provisions:** Provisions should be included that outline what will happen if a payment is not made on time or the loan is otherwise defaulted on, which can include late fees, acceleration of the remaining balance, or other penalties.
6. **Add signature and witnessing:** The note should be signed by both the borrower and the lender, and it should be witnessed by a neutral party.
7. **Make a copy:** Both parties should keep a copy of the note, and it is important to note that this is performed subsequently to signing.
