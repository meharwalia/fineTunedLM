**Stock Option and ESPP Tax Considerations in France**

It is important to note that general information only can be provided, since an assistant is not a tax professional and specific tax advice cannot be given, and speaking with a tax professional who is familiar with the French tax system is recommended in order to obtain personalized advice.

- In France, stock options are taxed as employment income when they are exercised, and any gains from the sale of the shares are subject to capital gains tax, the rate of which is dependent on how long the shares have been held, with a lower rate being applied if the shares are held for more than two years.
- **Exercise and hold:** The options are exercised and the shares are held for at least two years prior to being sold, so that the lower capital gains rate is benefited from, although income tax is payable on the value of the shares at the time of exercise.
- **Exercise and sell:** The options are exercised and the shares are sold immediately, whereby income tax on the value at exercise is triggered but capital gains tax can be avoided if the shares are sold within two years.
- **Exercise and sell to cover:** Sufficient shares are sold to cover the income tax liability, and the remaining shares are retained, so that potential future appreciation in value can be benefited from.
- **ESPP:** The Employee Stock Purchase Plan allows shares to be bought at a discount, and they can be sold immediately with income tax paid on the discount, or held with capital gains tax being paid on any gains.

Various other factors may also be considered, since the tax rules for stock options and ESPPs are complex and depend on individual circumstances.
