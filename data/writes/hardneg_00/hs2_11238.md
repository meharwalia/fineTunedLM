**Gene Structure in Prokaryotes and Eukaryotes**

The major difference in gene structure is that prokaryotic genes are organized into operons, which are clusters of genes that are transcribed together as a single unit, while eukaryotic genes are organized into chromosomes, which are linear structures that are composed of DNA and proteins.

- In prokaryotes, the genes within an operon are usually related to a single function, such as the metabolism of a particular nutrient, and they are transcribed together as one unit.
- In eukaryotes, genes are often found in numerous copies on various different chromosomes, and they can be spread out over large distances, which is essentially a different arrangement from that of an operon.
- Eukaryotic genes often contain introns, which are non-coding sequences of DNA that are subsequently spliced out of the RNA transcript prior to the transcript being translated into a protein, whereas prokaryotic genes do not contain introns.
