**Understanding Heuristics**

Heuristics are mental shortcuts or rules of thumb that are utilised by people to make decisions, solve problems, or form judgments quickly and efficiently, and they are based on past experiences, assumptions, and intuition rather than on a systematic analysis of all available information.

- Heuristics can be useful in numerous situations, since quick decisions are allowed without a lot of time and effort being spent on analysing all the details.
- Errors and biases can also be led to by heuristics, as important information may be overlooked or assumptions may be made that are not based on facts.
- Several common heuristics are recognised, including the availability heuristic (the likelihood of an event is estimated based on how easily examples come to mind), the representativeness heuristic (the likelihood of an event is judged based on how closely it resembles a stereotype or prototype), and the anchoring heuristic (an initial piece of information is relied on too heavily when subsequent judgments are made).
- It should be noted that heuristics are studied in psychology, particularly in judgment and decision-making, where they have been found to be used automatically and unconsciously and to have a significant impact on the decisions that are made.
