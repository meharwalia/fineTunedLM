**Step-by-Step Business Plan for a Relational Contracting and Collaborative Law Practice**

A business plan is a written document in which the goals, strategies, and financial projections of a business are outlined, and it should be prepared in accordance with the sections that are listed below.

1. **Executive Summary:** A brief overview of the business is written, in which the services that are offered, the target market, and the key financial goals are included.
2. **Company Description:** A detailed description is provided of the legal structure, ownership, and management team of the business.
3. **Market Analysis:** Research is conducted on the market for relational contracting and collaborative law legal services, including the target market, competition, and industry trends.
4. **Products and Services:** The specific legal services and value-added products or services that will be offered to clients are outlined.
5. **Marketing and Sales Strategy:** It should be explained how the business will be marketed and how sales will be generated, and the pricing strategy is included in this section.
6. **Management and Organization:** The structure of the business is described, including the roles and responsibilities of each member of the team.
7. **Financial Projections:** A financial plan is created that includes projected income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements for the first three years of business.
8. **Appendices:** Any additional information that supports the plan, such as resumes, licenses, and permits, is provided.

**Tips for Success**

- The plan should be kept concise and focused, with clear language being utilised so that it can be read easily.
- Thorough market research should be conducted to understand the needs of the target market and the competition.
- Financial projections should be based on realistic assumptions and potential risks and challenges should be taken into account.
- The value proposition should be focused on by explaining what makes the legal services unique.
- Pricing is considered by offering both fixed prices and billable hour fees, whereby fixed prices may be offered for simple, straightforward services and billable hours for more complex or time-consuming services.
- Feedback should be obtained from mentors, colleagues, or potential clients.
