**State of Bipartisan Politics in America**

The current state of bipartisan politics in America reflects significant polarization, with several key issues intensifying the divide, and while it is challenging to definitively say whether the nation is more divided than ever, the current political landscape is marked by heightened partisanship, especially when compared to more consensus-driven eras in the past, such as the post-World War II period.

1. **Immigration:** It should be noted that, contrary to the earlier stated figures, a reverse trend is shown by recent data, and a Pew Research Center survey indicates that immigration is viewed as a positive for the country by a significant majority of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents (76%), compared to 38% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents.
2. **Healthcare:** A stark partisan divide on government-run healthcare is revealed by a Kaiser Family Foundation poll, with 88% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents supporting it, contrasted with only 18% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents.
3. **Gun Control:** According to Pew Research Center, stricter gun laws are supported by approximately 86% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents, while they are favored by a minority of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents (31%).
4. **Climate Change:** Climate change is perceived as a major threat by 83% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents, in stark contrast to 27% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents, according to Pew Research Center data.
5. **Abortion:** A Pew Research Center survey notes, prior to which various figures were cited, that abortion is believed by 80% of Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents to be legal in all or most cases, whereas this view is shared by only 38% of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents.

It should be noted that partisan perspectives are shown by these issues to shape policy debates in America, and this division is not unprecedented in American history, but the level of polarization, which is partly fueled by social media and the 24-hour news cycle, presents unique challenges, while the increasing politicization of cultural and social issues is contributed to by Congress reflecting these growing partisan differences.

In summary, while periods of division have always been experienced by bipartisan politics in America, the current era is marked by significant polarization on key issues, and the extent of these divisions is clarified by accurate data from reputable sources like Pew Research Center and Kaiser Family Foundation, whereby a clearer picture, utilised by numerous observers, of the contemporary political landscape is offered.
