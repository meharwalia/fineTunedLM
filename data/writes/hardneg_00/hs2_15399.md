**Explanation of the Monty Hall Problem**

The Monty Hall problem is a classic probability puzzle that can be a bit tricky to understand at first, and it involves three doors, behind one of which a prize is placed and behind the other two of which goats are placed, and a door is chosen by the player before another door is opened by the host (Monty Hall) to reveal a goat.

- The player is subsequently given the option to switch the choice to the remaining unopened door or to stick with the original choice, and it should be noted that switching is the better strategy.
- Prior to the host opening another door, all three doors have an equal chance of containing the prize, so the probability of winning by sticking with the original choice is 1/3.
- After the host opens another door to reveal a goat, the probability of the prize being behind the original choice remains 1/3, while the probability of it being behind the remaining unopened door is increased to 2/3.
- This is because only two doors are left, and one of them has been eliminated by the host by a goat being shown, so that the probability of the prize being behind the remaining unopened door is twice as high as the probability of it being behind the original choice.
