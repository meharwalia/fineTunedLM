BOE (barrels of oil equivalent) and BO (barrels of oil) are two units in petroleum engineering. Both measure the amount of oil and natural gas in a given resource. They differ in 4 ways.

1. Definition: BOE expresses the amount of oil and natural gas in a given resource. You calculate it by converting the volume of oil to equivalent barrels of natural gas, which is a more widely used unit in the industry. BOE is a standardized measure of the amount of oil and natural gas in a reservoir.
2. Calculation: Divide the total volume of oil and natural gas in a reservoir by the conversion ratio. The conversion ratio is typically 6:1. This means that 6 cubic feet of natural gas equal 1 barrel of oil. The result is in BOE.
3. BO (barrels of oil): BO measures the amount of oil in a given resource. It is the most commonly used unit in the petroleum industry for the quantity of crude oil. 1 barrel of oil equals 42 gallons of liquid oil.
4. Comparison: BOE and BO measure different units. BOE is a standardized measure of the amount of oil and natural gas in a reservoir. BO measures the amount of oil alone. You can use the two units together to get a comprehensive understanding of the resource.

Both units are important in the petroleum industry. They evaluate the size and potential of oil and natural gas reserves.
