DLT and blockchain are both distributed ledger technologies. They differ in 4 ways.

- **Data structure:** A blockchain is a specific type of DLT. It uses a linked list data structure to store a growing list of records, called blocks. Each block has a timestamp and a link to the previous block. DLT can use other data structures, such as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) or a merkle tree.
- **Access control:** Blockchains are typically public and permissionless. Anyone can join the network and verify transactions without permission. DLTs can also be public and permissionless. They can also be private and permissioned, with access restricted to specified participants.
- **Consensus mechanism:** Blockchains often use cryptographic proof-of-work (PoW) or proof-of-stake (PoS) algorithms. These algorithms reach consensus and prevent double-spending. DLTs can use other consensus algorithms, such as Byzantine fault tolerance (BFT) or consensus-as-a-service (CaaS).
- **Flexibility:** Blockchains typically have a fixed set of rules and functions. You cannot change them after deployment. DLTs are more flexible. They allow smart contracts and decentralized applications (dApps) that run on the network.

Both technologies use distributed ledgers to record transactions.
