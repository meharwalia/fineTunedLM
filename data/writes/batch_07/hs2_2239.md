The answer depends on the gas biomarkers of the disease and the limit of detection that the sensor needs. Different diseases need different levels of sensitivity. This depends on the concentration of the gas biomarkers in breath or other samples.

These diseases have distinct gas biomarker profiles:

1. Lung cancer: Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) such as acetone, ethanol and isoprene are potential breath biomarkers.
2. Colorectal cancer: VOCs such as acetone, ethanol and methyl ketones are potential breath biomarkers.
3. Liver disease: Breath samples of patients have higher levels of ammonia and hydrogen sulfide than healthy individuals.
4. Diabetes: Breath samples of patients have higher levels of acetone and methanol than healthy individuals.

When you select a disease, consider its gas biomarkers and the limit of detection that the sensor needs. A disease can need a lower limit of detection than another disease. This depends on the concentration of the gas biomarkers in breath or other samples.

Also consider if you can collect breath samples. Consider the potential for false positives or negatives with a metal oxide semiconductor sensor.
