A language that is universally easy to learn is a challenging goal. People have different linguistic backgrounds and learning styles. These 7 factors apply to a concept for the easiest language to learn.

1. Simplicity: The language has a simple grammar and vocabulary. It has clear rules for word order and sentence structure. This helps learners understand and remember the language.
2. Familiarity: The language has elements that people from different linguistic backgrounds know. For example, it can borrow words from widely spoken languages such as English, Spanish and Chinese.
3. Visual cues: The language can include visual cues, such as gestures and facial expressions. They help learners understand the meaning of words and phrases.
4. Phonetic clarity: The language has a clear, consistent phonetic system. Its sounds are easy to produce and recognize.
5. Online resources: The language has a comprehensive set of online resources. These include learning materials, dictionaries and translation tools. They make it easier for learners to access and use the language.
6. Cultural neutrality: The language avoids cultural references and stereotypes. They can offend or confuse learners from different backgrounds.
7. Global adoption: People from different countries and cultures widely adopt and accept the language. This makes it easier for people to communicate with each other.

The easiest language to learn is simple, familiar, visual and phonetically clear. It has comprehensive online resources. It is also culturally neutral and widely adopted around the world.
