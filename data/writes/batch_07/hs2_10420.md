A business plan is a written document. It outlines the goals, strategies and financial projections of a business.

**Steps to create the plan**

1. Executive Summary: Write a brief overview of your business. Include the services you offer, your target market and your key financial goals.
2. Company Description: Describe your business in detail. Include the legal structure, ownership and management team.
3. Market Analysis: Research the market for relational contracting and collaborative law legal services. Include the target market, competition and industry trends.
4. Products and Services: Outline the specific legal services and value-added products or services you offer to clients.
5. Marketing and Sales Strategy: Explain how you market your business and generate sales. Include your pricing strategy.
6. Management and Organization: Describe the structure of your business. Include the roles and responsibilities of each team member.
7. Financial Projections: Create a financial plan. Include projected income statements, balance sheets and cash flow statements for the first 3 years of business.
8. Appendices: Add information that supports your plan, such as resumes, licenses and permits.

**Tips for success**

1. Keep your plan concise and focused. It must be easy to read, with clear language.
2. Do your research. Research the market thoroughly to understand the needs of your target market and the competition in your industry.
3. Be realistic. Base your financial projections on realistic assumptions. Consider potential risks and challenges.
4. Focus on your value proposition. Explain what makes your legal services unique and why clients choose your business.
5. Consider your pricing strategy. Offer both fixed prices and billable hour fees, depending on the type of legal service or value-added product or service. For example, offer fixed prices for simple, straightforward services. Use billable hours for more complex or time-consuming services.
6. Seek feedback. Share your plan with mentors, colleagues or potential clients. Ask for feedback to improve your plan.
