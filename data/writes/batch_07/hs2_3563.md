Syro-Malabar culture is a unique cultural tradition that developed over time in the state of Kerala in India. It blends Hindu, Christian and Islamic influences. This reflects the diverse religious background of the region.

The culture mainly belongs to the Syro-Malabar Catholic Church. This Catholic Church in India follows the Eastern rite. It has a long history in the region, from the 4th century.

The church has played a significant role in shaping the culture and traditions of the area.

People know the culture for its rich artistic and literary traditions, including classical music, dance and literature. They also know it for its unique cuisine, which uses spices and other ingredients. Traditional crafts, such as hand-woven textiles and woodcarvings, are another feature.

Festivals and celebrations are the most distinctive aspect of the culture. They are an important part of community life. Popular festivals include Onam and Christmas.

- Onam is a harvest festival. It celebrates the return of the legendary king Mahabali.
- Christmas has great enthusiasm and joy across the region.

Syro-Malabar culture is a rich, vibrant tradition. It reflects the diverse history and religious background of the region.

It is an important part of the cultural heritage of Kerala. The people of the area celebrate and preserve it.
