Large language models (LLMs) have 6 potential use cases in investigative journalism.

1. Text analysis: LLMs analyze large amounts of text data, such as news articles, court documents and other sources of information. They identify patterns, trends and relationships in the data. This helps journalists find stories and insights that they can otherwise miss.
2. Data visualization: LLMs create visual representations of data, such as charts, graphs and maps. This helps journalists present complex data in a more accessible, understandable way. Readers understand the information more easily.
3. Text summarization: LLMs summarize large amounts of text data. This reduces the time and effort to review and analyze the data. It is particularly useful for journalists who must quickly review large amounts of information, such as for a breaking news story.
4. Fact-checking: LLMs verify the accuracy of information in text sources. They compare statements and check the information. This helps journalists base their stories on accurate, reliable sources.
5. Language processing: LLMs process and analyze language through translation, transcription and text classification. This is useful for journalists who must translate documents. It is also useful to analyze language and understand the context and meaning of the text.
6. Story generation: LLMs generate stories and content from a given prompt or topic. This is useful for journalists who must quickly generate content or ideas. It is also useful to generate content ideas for stories.

LLMs are a powerful tool for investigative journalists. They help journalists quickly analyze large amounts of text data. They also help journalists generate insights and stories that they can otherwise miss.
