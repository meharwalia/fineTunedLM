The U.S. has more than two political parties. The two major parties are the Democratic Party and the Republican Party. They dominate because of the first-past-the-post electoral system, the two-party system, and the influence of money and special interests.

You can create more political parties, and there have been many attempts. But it is difficult for new parties to gain traction and compete with the established parties.

No legal restrictions stop the creation of new political parties in the United States. But practical barriers make it difficult for new parties to gain recognition and support:

- Because of the two-party system, candidates from smaller parties struggle to gain media attention and funding.
- The first-past-the-post system makes it difficult for third-party candidates to win. They often split the vote, and the major party candidates win.

In recent years, there have been efforts to reform the electoral system and create a more multi-party system. Examples are ranked-choice voting and proportional representation. These efforts have not yet succeeded.
