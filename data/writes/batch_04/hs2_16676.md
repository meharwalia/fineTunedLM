Fifteen types of courts exist in the legal system. Each has its own jurisdiction, purpose, and procedures.

1. **Supreme Court:** The highest court in a country or state. It handles appeals and constitutional issues. Example: The United States Supreme Court.
2. **Appellate Court:** It reviews decisions of lower courts. It does not reexamine facts. It evaluates legal issues. Example: United States Court of Appeals.
3. **Trial Court:** Legal actions begin here. It involves presentation of evidence and examination of witnesses. Example: District Court.
4. **Military Court:** It handles legal matters in the military, including disciplinary actions and violations of military law. Example: Court-martial.
5. **Bankruptcy Court:** It deals with cases of bankruptcy and financial insolvency. Example: United States Bankruptcy Court.
6. **International Court of Justice (ICJ):** It resolves legal disputes between nations based on international law. Example: ICJ at The Hague.
7. **Tribal Court:** It deals with legal matters in Native American reservations and applies tribal laws. Example: Navajo Nation Tribal Court.
8. **Family Court:** It deals with family matters such as divorce, child custody, and domestic violence. Example: Family Court in multiple jurisdictions.
9. **Probate Court:** It handles matters of wills, estates, and guardianship. Example: Probate Court in different states.
10. **Small Claims Court:** It resolves disputes about small amounts of money without extensive legal procedures. Example: Small Claims Court in multiple states.
11. **Traffic Court:** It adjudicates cases of traffic violations and offenses. Example: Municipal Traffic Court.
12. **Juvenile Court:** It focuses on cases of minors, such as delinquency and dependency. Example: Juvenile Court in different jurisdictions.
13. **Labor Court:** It addresses disputes between employers and employees about labor laws and regulations. Example: National Labor Relations Board.
14. **Tax Court:** It deals with disputes about tax laws and regulations. Example: United States Tax Court.
15. **Administrative Court:** It reviews decisions of government agencies and makes sure they comply with the law. Example: Environmental Appeals Board.

These courts follow specific rules and procedures. This gives a fair resolution of legal issues in their jurisdictions.

The hierarchy of courts allows appeals and review of decisions. This gives a system of checks and balances in the legal process.
