A biometric system is a technology. It uses biological characteristics of a person to identify them or verify their identity. These characteristics are biometric traits.

They are unique to each person. They include facial features, fingerprints, iris patterns, voice, and palm veins.

Biometric systems are used in access control, law enforcement, border control, and healthcare. In access control, a fingerprint or iris pattern can give a person access to a secure area or building. In law enforcement, biometric systems can identify suspects or missing persons.

Biometric systems have advantages over traditional identification, such as passwords or ID cards:

- Biometric traits are unique and difficult to replicate or steal. This makes identification more secure.
- They are convenient. You do not need passwords or other forms of identification.

Biometric systems also raise concerns about privacy and security:

- Hackers can steal biometric data, or others can use the data for unauthorized purposes.
- Some people are uncomfortable when systems collect and store their biological data.
