Inventory moves from the balance sheet to the P&L (Profit and Loss) statement when the business sells it. Here are three levels of detail.

**Level 1: High-level overview**

- Inventory is the stock of goods that a business holds for sale to customers.
- The balance sheet shows inventory as a current asset. Its value is the lower of cost or market value. It can include the buying cost, transportation costs, and other associated costs.
- When the business sells inventory, the inventory balance on the balance sheet decreases. The P&L statement records the sales revenue.
- The P&L statement also records the cost of goods sold (COGS). COGS includes the cost of the inventory sold and associated costs, such as shipping and handling.
- Gross profit is sales revenue minus COGS. It is a key metric of profitability.

**Level 2: More detail**

- When the business receives inventory, the balance sheet records it as a current asset.
- The P&L statement records the associated costs as expenses. These are the buying cost, transportation costs, and other associated costs.
- When the business sells inventory, the inventory balance on the balance sheet decreases. The P&L statement records the sales revenue.
- The P&L statement records the cost of the inventory sold as COGS, with associated costs such as shipping and handling.
- Gross profit is sales revenue minus COGS. It is a key metric of profitability.

**Level 3: Even more detail**

- When the business receives inventory, the balance sheet records it as a current asset.
- The P&L statement records the associated costs as expenses. These are the buying cost, transportation costs, and other associated costs.
- When the business sells inventory, the inventory balance on the balance sheet decreases. The P&L statement records the sales revenue.
- The P&L statement records the cost of the inventory sold as COGS, with associated costs such as shipping and handling.
- Gross profit is sales revenue minus COGS. It is a key metric of profitability.

The movement of inventory through the balance sheet and P&L shows the cash flow, profitability, and performance of the company. With this knowledge, businesses can make informed decisions about operations and financial strategies.
