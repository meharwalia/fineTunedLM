Domain-driven design (DDD) is a software development methodology. It solves complex business problems by aligning software with a domain model.

Eric Evans introduced it in 2004. It is now a popular approach for enterprise applications.

The core idea: a deep understanding of the domain is essential for a successful software solution. The domain is the problem that the software system solves.

DDD bridges the gap between business and technology. Domain experts join the development process, and both sides use a common language.

**Key concepts**

- **Domain model:** A representation of the business domain as objects, entities, services, and value objects. It captures the rules, constraints, and behavior of the domain.
- **Ubiquitous language:** A shared vocabulary between business experts and developers. It communicates the concepts of the domain and makes sure the software represents the domain accurately.
- **Domain events:** Events in the domain that have significance. They can trigger actions or update the domain state.
- **Aggregates:** Groups of related entities that the system manages as a unit. They have their own identity and behavior.
- **Repository:** An interface that gives uniform access to domain objects and persists them. It hides the details of the persistence mechanism.
- **Services:** High-level, domain-specific services. They perform business logic and interact with the domain model.

DDD is a complex topic with more concepts and patterns. The key idea is to use a deep understanding of the domain to guide software design and development. The result is a system that represents the domain accurately and solves the business problem effectively.
