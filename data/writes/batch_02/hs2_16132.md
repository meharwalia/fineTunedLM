The three taxonomic domains of life are Archaea, Bacteria, and Eukarya. They share a common ancestor but have distinct characteristics.

**Archaea and Bacteria**

Both are single-celled prokaryotic organisms. They have no nucleus and no other membrane-bound organelles.

They have a single, circular chromosome of DNA in the cytoplasm. Members of both groups can survive in extreme environments, such as high salt concentrations, high temperatures, and low pH.

They differ in these ways:

- Both have a cell membrane and cell wall, but the composition differs. Bacteria typically have a peptidoglycan cell wall. Archaea have a unique cell wall structure with ether lipids.
- The lipids of Archaea have ether bonds. Bacteria have ester bonds.
- Their metabolic processes differ. For instance, some archaea use methanogenesis to generate methane gas.

Both use ribosomes for protein synthesis.

**Eukarya**

Eukaryotes are more complex and typically bigger than prokaryotic organisms. They are often multicellular. They have a true nucleus that houses their DNA.

They include organisms from single-celled yeasts to multicellular plants and animals. Eukaryotes also have membrane-bound organelles for specific functions. Mitochondria produce energy.

Chloroplasts perform photosynthesis in plants.

**Shared characteristics**

All three domains share these characteristics:

- **Genetic material:** They all have DNA as genetic material. They use the same basic process of DNA replication to reproduce.
- **Protein synthesis:** They all use the same basic process of protein synthesis. They use RNA and DNA to make proteins.
- **Cellular structure:** They all have a plasma membrane. It separates the inside of the cell from the outside environment.
- **Energy source:** They all use ATP as their primary energy source.
