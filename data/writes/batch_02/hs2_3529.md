The rules of chess are complex. Here is a brief overview:

1. Each player has 16 pieces: 1 king, 1 queen, 2 rooks, 2 knights, 2 bishops, and 8 pawns.
2. The objective is to checkmate the opponent's king. Checkmate means the king is under attack (in check) and has no way to move out of harm's way.
3. Each piece moves differently:
   - The king moves one square in any direction.
   - The queen moves any number of squares in any direction.
   - The rook moves any number of squares along the same row or column.
   - The knight moves in an L-shape pattern.
   - The bishop moves any number of squares along the diagonals.
   - The pawn moves one square forward. It can capture pieces that are diagonally in front of it.
4. If a player's king is in danger of checkmate, the player can put the opponent's king in check. This distracts the opponent from the player's own king.
5. A player makes only one move at a time. A piece cannot move into a square that holds a piece of the same player.
6. If a player's king is in check, the player must move the king out of harm's way or capture the attacking piece.
7. A player can make a special move called "castling." The king moves two squares toward a rook. The rook then moves to the square that the king just crossed.
8. If a player's king is in check and the player cannot move the king or capture the attacking piece, the player must resign.
9. The game can also end in a draw. This happens if neither player can make a legal move, or if both players agree to a draw.

These are the basic rules of chess. More details and variations affect the strategy of the game.
