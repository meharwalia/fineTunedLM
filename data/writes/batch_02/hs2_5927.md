Here is a leaflet about HIV (human immunodeficiency virus).

**About HIV**

HIV is a virus that damages the cells in your immune system. It weakens your ability to fight every infection and disease.

**Definition**

HIV attacks the body's immune system, specifically the white blood cells called T-cells.

**Signs and symptoms**

Early symptoms may include fever, fatigue, rash, and swollen lymph nodes. As the disease progresses, severe symptoms may develop. These include weight loss, night sweats, and recurrent infections.

People with HIV often have no symptoms for years. For this reason, get tested regularly.

**Cause and transmission**

HIV spreads through contact with the blood, semen, breast milk, or vaginal fluids of an infected person. The most common ways of transmission are:

- Unprotected sexual intercourse (vaginal or anal) with an infected partner
- Sharing needles or other injection equipment with an infected person
- Mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy, delivery, or breastfeeding

HIV does not spread through casual contact. Examples are shaking hands, hugging, sharing food or drinks, and being coughed or sneezed on.

**Treatment**

There is no cure for HIV. Effective treatments control the virus and prevent damage to the immune system. The treatments use a combination of antiretroviral drugs.

These drugs can reduce the amount of virus in the body to undetectable levels. This improves the person's health. It also reduces the risk of transmitting the virus to others.

**Prevention**

The best way to prevent HIV is to avoid exposure to the virus. To reduce the risk of transmission:

- Always use condoms during sexual intercourse, especially with multiple partners or high-risk behaviors.
- Do not share needles or other injection equipment. If you use drugs, get clean needles from a trusted source.
- If you are pregnant, get prenatal care. Avoid breastfeeding if possible. If you must breastfeed, express breast milk manually. Do not use a breast pump.
- Get tested regularly for HIV and other sexually transmitted infections. Knowing your status helps you protect yourself and others.

**Outlook**

HIV is a serious virus. Untreated, it can have devastating consequences.

With early detection and proper treatment, people with HIV can live long and healthy lives. Prevent transmission and get tested regularly to help stop the spread of this disease.
