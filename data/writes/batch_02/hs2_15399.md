Switching doors gives a 2/3 chance of winning. Here is the reason.

The Monty Hall problem has three doors. The prize is behind one door. Goats are behind the other two.

You choose a door. Before you open it, the host (Monty Hall) opens another door to show a goat. You can then switch to the remaining unopened door or keep your original choice.

Before the host opens a door, all three doors have an equal chance of holding the prize. Your original choice has a 1/3 probability of winning.

After the host opens a door to show a goat, the probability that the prize is behind your original door stays at 1/3. The probability that the prize is behind the remaining unopened door rises to 2/3.

The host has eliminated one door by showing a goat. The remaining unopened door is now twice as likely to hold the prize as your original door.

Therefore, switch to the remaining unopened door. This raises your chance of winning from 1/3 to 2/3. The probability changes as the host reveals more information.
