A difference-in-differences model with one year of post-treatment data may not give a reliable estimate of the policy's impact. With one year of post-treatment data, it is hard to account for long-term effects of the policy. It is also hard to account for trends or seasonality in the data.

Consider these two approaches:

1. Baseline trend. Use the pre-treatment data to estimate a baseline trend for the outcome variable. Then compare the observed post-treatment outcome to this baseline trend. Use a regression model with a dummy variable for the post-treatment period and a term for the baseline trend. The coefficient on the post-treatment dummy variable estimates the impact of the policy.
2. Propensity score matching. Use the pre-treatment data to estimate the probability that a unit is treated (subject to the policy), based on observable characteristics. Then match treated units with control units by their estimated propensity scores. Compare the outcomes of the treated and control units in the post-treatment period. This helps account for differences in observable characteristics between treated and control units that may affect their outcomes.

With both approaches, consider carefully the assumptions and limitations of the methods. Run sensitivity analyses to test how robust the results are to alternative assumptions.
