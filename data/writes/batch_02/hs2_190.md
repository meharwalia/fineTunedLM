Scientists debate which organism was the first with sensory organs. Sensory organs evolved over millions of years. The oldest known fossils of organisms with sensory organs are about 550 million years old.

They belong to ancient marine animals called the Ediacaran biota. The fossils include trace fossils, such as tracks and burrows. These suggest sensory organs such as eyes and ears.

One of the earliest known animals with well-developed sensory organs is the trilobite of the Cambrian period. It had compound eyes. It likely had well-developed senses of sight and touch, and possibly hearing.

Other ancient animals with sensory organs include:

- Cephalopods. They have sophisticated eyes and likely have advanced vision.
- Echinoderms. Their sensory organs, called tube feet, sense touch, pressure, and chemical cues in the environment.

Scientists do not definitely know the first organism with sensory organs. The fossil record suggests that sensory organs evolved early in the history of life on Earth. They helped early organisms survive and succeed.
