Here is a sample curriculum for the communication skills in your job description:

1. Introduction to Communication Skills: Students learn why effective communication matters in the workplace. They learn the types of communication: verbal, nonverbal, and written. They explore their own communication styles and identify areas to improve.
2. Verbal Communication: This module covers clear and concise speaking, active listening, and appropriate language. Students practice giving feedback, asking questions, and presenting information.
3. Nonverbal Communication: Body language, facial expressions, and tone of voice affect how others see us. Students learn to use nonverbal cues effectively and to interpret the nonverbal signals of others.
4. Written Communication: Effective written communication is essential in the workplace. Students learn to write clear and concise emails, reports, and other documents. They also practice editing and proofreading their own work and giving feedback to others.
5. Cultural Awareness: Cultural differences influence communication. Students learn cultural norms and expectations for communication. They learn to adapt their communication style to different cultural contexts.
6. Conflict Resolution: Conflicts are inevitable in any workplace. Students learn to identify and resolve conflicts effectively. They practice active listening, empathy, and problem-solving skills to resolve conflicts constructively.
7. Presentation Skills: Jobs often require presentations to colleagues, clients, or other stakeholders. Students learn to prepare and deliver effective presentations. They learn to create visual aids and handle questions and feedback from the audience.
8. Professionalism: This module covers professionalism in the workplace. Topics include how to dress appropriately, how to keep a positive attitude, and how to handle difficult situations with grace.
9. Networking: Networking builds relationships and advances a career. Students learn to network effectively. They learn to introduce themselves, engage in conversations, and follow up with contacts.
10. Summary and Reflection: Students reflect on what they learned. They identify areas where they want to keep developing their communication skills. They also get feedback on their progress and performance from peers and the instructor.

This curriculum covers a wide range of important workplace communication skills. Customize it to fit the needs of your students and the requirements of your job.
