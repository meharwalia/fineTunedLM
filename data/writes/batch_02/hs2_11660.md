Yes, American residents and American citizens are different for estate taxes.

**Citizens**

American citizens pay estate tax on their worldwide assets, regardless of where they live. The estate tax is a tax on the transfer of the assets of a person after death.

As of 2021, the federal estate tax exemption is $11.7 million. The estate of a citizen who dies in 2021 with assets of more than $11.7 million is subject to estate tax.

**Residents who are not citizens**

Residents who are not U.S. citizens also pay estate tax, but only on their assets in the United States. If a non-U.S. citizen resident dies, the estate pays estate tax on U.S.-situs assets. These generally include real property in the United States, tangible personal property in the United States, and stock in U.S. corporations.

The rules on estate taxes are complex. Other factors can apply to your specific circumstances. Consult a qualified tax professional or estate planning attorney to make sure you comply with the law.
