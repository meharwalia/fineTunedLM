Nobel Prize-winning physicist Enrico Fermi asked why we have found no other civilization. The universe is old, and it has a vast number of planets that could support life. His question led to the Fermi paradox.

Three possible reasons explain why we have not found aliens:

1. The universe is vast and the distances are huge. Two civilizations have a low chance of meeting.
2. Aliens may exist, but our technology cannot detect their life forms or activities. Their life forms could differ from life on Earth.
3. Aliens and humans could use different methods of communication and different technologies. We could miss each other's signals.

The Great Filter is another concept. Robin Hanson formulated this theory.

It says that interstellar life forms must clear a series of hurdles to become an advanced spacefaring civilization. Perhaps we have not cleared at least one of them.

The hurdles include these stages:

- Life forms form and develop from simple to complex.
- Life forms evolve into advanced intelligent beings.
- Beings create advanced technologies.
- Beings colonize the universe.
- Beings avoid self-destruction.

We may be alone in the universe. We may also not have met intelligent life yet. The search continues.
