Diabetes is a chronic medical condition. It affects the ability of the body to regulate blood glucose (sugar) levels. The two main types are Type 1 and Type 2.

**Type 1 diabetes**

Type 1 diabetes is also known as juvenile diabetes or insulin-dependent diabetes. It is an autoimmune condition. The immune system attacks and destroys the insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas.

The body then lacks insulin, a hormone that helps the body use glucose for energy. People with Type 1 diabetes need insulin injections or an insulin pump to manage their blood glucose levels.

**Type 2 diabetes**

Type 2 diabetes is more common. It typically develops in adulthood. The body becomes resistant to the action of insulin, so it does not use insulin effectively.

This causes high blood glucose levels. Untreated, this can lead to health problems. Lifestyle changes, such as a healthy diet and regular exercise, can often manage Type 2 diabetes.

In some cases, the person also needs medication or insulin therapy.

**Gestational diabetes**

Gestational diabetes can develop during pregnancy in women who do not have diabetes. It usually goes away after the baby is born. It can increase the risk of Type 2 diabetes later in life.

**Symptoms and complications**

Untreated diabetes can cause these symptoms and complications:

- Frequent urination
- Increased thirst and hunger
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores
- Nerve damage

People with diabetes must monitor their blood glucose levels. They must work with their healthcare provider to manage their condition effectively.
