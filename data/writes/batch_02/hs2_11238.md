The major difference is the way each group groups its genes.

- Prokaryotic genes form operons. An operon is a cluster of genes that cells transcribe together as a single unit. The genes in an operon usually relate to a single function, such as the metabolism of a particular nutrient.
- Eukaryotic genes sit on chromosomes. Chromosomes are linear structures of DNA and proteins. Eukaryotic genes are often in multiple copies on different chromosomes. They can lie far apart.

Eukaryotic genes also often contain introns. Introns are non-coding sequences of DNA. The cell splices them out of the RNA transcript before it translates the transcript into a protein. Prokaryotic genes do not contain introns.
