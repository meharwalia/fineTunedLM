Turkish politics has a recent history shaped by major political and social changes.

**Background**

In the 20th century, Turkey changed from the Ottoman Empire to a modern republic. Mustafa Kemal Atatürk founded the Turkish Republic. He established a secular and democratic system based on nationalism, populism, and statism.

**Parties**

Since the 1950s, two main parties have dominated Turkish politics. They are the center-left Republican People's Party (CHP) and the center-right Justice and Development Party (AKP).

The CHP was the dominant party until the 1990s. Then the AKP became a major player.

**Recent years**

Turkey has faced political and economic challenges. These include a coup attempt in 2016 and a growing rift between the government and the opposition. The country has also seen a crackdown on civil liberties and more authoritarianism under President Recep Tayyip Erdogan.

**Political system**

Turkey is a presidential republic. The president is the head of state.

The prime minister is the head of government. Turkey adopted its current constitution in 2017, after a controversial referendum that gave the president greater powers.

**Recent elections**

The AKP has been the largest party in parliament since 2002. Erdogan has been president since 2014.

In the June 2018 general election, the AKP and its ally, the Nationalist Movement Party (MHP), won most seats in parliament. The opposition and international observers criticized the election for irregularities and a lack of transparency.

Historical and cultural factors have shaped Turkish politics, which is complex. The country faces political and economic challenges. The political system is becoming more authoritarian under President Erdogan.
