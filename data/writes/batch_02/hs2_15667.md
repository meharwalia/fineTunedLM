Scientists cannot yet explain five things about our universe:

1. Dark matter: Dark matter does not emit, absorb, or reflect light, so telescopes cannot see it. Scientists believe it makes up about 85% of the matter in the universe. They infer its existence from its gravitational effects on visible matter and from the rotation of galaxies.
2. Dark energy: Scientists think dark energy causes the observed acceleration of the expansion of the universe. They believe it makes up about 68% of the energy in the universe. Its nature is unknown, and its properties are hard to measure.
3. The origin of the universe: Research and debate on this topic continue. The leading theory is the Big Bang theory. It suggests that the universe began as a single point of infinite density and temperature about 13.8 billion years ago. Questions remain about the early moments of the universe and the conditions that led to its creation.
4. The fine-tuning of the universe: The physical constants and laws of the universe appear finely tuned to allow life. For example, the strength of the gravitational force and the size of the universe allow stars and planets to form. Scientists still debate and speculate about this fine-tuning.
5. The fate of the universe: Research and debate on this topic also continue. The current understanding is that the universe expands at an accelerating rate. This suggests it will expand forever. Theories about the far future include the Big Rip, Big Crunch, and Big Bounce.

New discoveries may help scientists solve these mysteries. So may measurements and observations with higher precision.
