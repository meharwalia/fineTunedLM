The Acts of Union were two parliamentary acts that the English and Scottish parliaments passed in 1707. England and Scotland were the countries involved. Negotiations and proposals lasted more than ten years.

The idea of a union first came up in 1689. The Scottish Parliament offered the crown to William and Mary, who already ruled England, on condition that they agree to a union. The negotiations failed because of disagreements over trade, taxation, and representation.

**Political motivation**

Both countries wanted to secure their mutual interests against France. France was a rival power in Europe.

It had supported the Jacobite rebellions, which aimed to restore the Catholic Stuart dynasty to the throne. The union would also end the chance of different monarchs ruling the two kingdoms, as happened during the Commonwealth period.

**Economic motivation**

Both countries wanted access to each other's markets and resources. They also wanted to benefit from the trade and commerce of the British Empire. Scotland had suffered a severe financial crisis after the Darien scheme failed.

This ambitious colonial project in Central America had drained much of its capital and population. The union would let Scotland share in the profits of English overseas ventures and recover from its losses.

**Religious motivation**

Both countries wanted to preserve the Protestant faith against Catholic influence and persecution. Both had experienced religious turmoil and civil wars in the previous century. Each had established a different form of Protestantism as its official religion.

The union would guarantee the security and freedom of the Anglican Church in England and the Presbyterian Church in Scotland. It would also prevent the return of Catholicism as the state religion.

**Challenges**

Both countries had to overcome the opposition and resentment of their populations, who had a long history of animosity and conflict. People in both countries feared that the union would erode their national identity, sovereignty, and culture. They feared that the other partner would dominate and exploit them.

Political factions and interest groups also resisted. These included the Tories in England and the Jacobites in Scotland, who had different agendas and visions for their countries.

**Result**

Both parliaments ratified the Acts of Union in 1707. They came into effect on May 1 of that year. They created a new state, the Kingdom of Great Britain. It united the kingdoms of England and Scotland under a single parliament and crown. The British Parliament first met in October 1707. Queen Anne, the last of the Stuart dynasty, was the first monarch of the new state. The union changed the political, economic, and religious landscape of the British Isles. It had lasting consequences for both countries.
