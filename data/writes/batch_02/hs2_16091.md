Sociology and anthropology are related fields, but they study complex social and cultural dynamics in different ways.

**Approach**

- Sociologists investigate the structure of groups, organizations, and societies. They also study how people interact in these contexts. (*American Sociological Association*)
- Anthropologists take a broad approach to understand the human experience. According to the American Anthropological Association, this includes human biology, archaeology, cultural anthropology, and linguistics.

**Research methods**

Sociologists view human dynamics as inherently social. Their research methods try to collect data about how social groups interact. These methods include participant observation, interviews, surveys, secondary analysis, experiments, and archival research. (*Journal of Sociological Methods & Research*)

Anthropologists study the human race as a whole, particularly its origins, development, and customs. Their research methods use theoretical frameworks and tools from multiple disciplines, including sociology.

They also use material observation, biological observation, and ethnographic fieldwork. These examine the social and cultural processes that shape human behavior, social relationships, and cultural practices. (*Research Methods in Anthropology*)

**Latest findings and insights**

- Social media shapes social and cultural dynamics. This includes the spread of misinformation and the formation of online communities.
- Power and inequality shape social and cultural dynamics. This includes the impact of race, class, and gender on social relationships and cultural practices. In a study published August 8, 2023 in the journal Sociology of Race and Ethnicity, sociologist Jose Loya examined how the mortgage refinance market perpetuates and shapes ethno-racial inequality and debt stratification. Loya used data analysis to show trends in lending disparity. These suggest structured inequality.
- Historical and political contexts shape social and cultural dynamics. This includes the impact of colonialism and post-colonialism on local cultures and communities. A 2019 archeological excavation in St. Croix, U.S. Virgin Islands sheds light on the lives of enslaved Africans. They lived and worked on the Estate Little Princess plantation from 1749 until 1848. Archeologists investigate how slavery shaped the lives of the Africans, the environmental features of the island, and the culture.
- Environmental factors enforce social and cultural dynamics. This includes the impact of climate change and disasters. Sociologists and anthropologists study how multiple, intersectional approaches better analyze and critique socioeconomic inequality. Researchers examine the root cause of vulnerability. They highlight systemic patterns that put particular people at more risk in a climate disaster. (*United Nations Women's Resilience to Disasters*)
