Bertie Wooster, the amiable and bumbling aristocrat, had heard of a new-fangled thing called "Twitter" from Jeeves, his ever-resourceful gentleman's gentleman. Bertie liked the idea of sharing his thoughts with the world in seconds. He decided to give it a go.

He sat at his desk, adjusted his monocle, and typed in the web address that Jeeves had given him. After false starts and colorful language, he created an account and logged in.

The homepage overwhelmed him. It held tweets about politics, sports, fashion, and everything in between. Bertie resolved to make the most of this new medium.

He began a witty observation about the weather. Words failed him. He had never been one for brevity, and the 140-character limit was a challenge.

After repeated tries, he wrote a tweet that satisfied him: "Weather's beastly today. Wish I was somewhere warmer. Perhaps I'll take a trip to the Riviera. #firstworldproblems"

He hit the "Tweet" button and watched his words go out into the ether. He was amazed at how easy it was. He could not wait to find more gems.

As he explored, Bertie found that Twitter was a treasure trove of humor, wit, and absurdity. He chuckled at the clever puns and rolled his eyes at the ridiculous hashtags.

In the end, Bertie saw that Twitter let him connect with others and find levity in a dreary world. For that, he was grateful.
